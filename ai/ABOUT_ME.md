# About the Engineer

This profile gives AI contributors enough context to collaborate with Jannsen
Agustin. It is not a résumé and should not be copied into public-facing content as
career marketing.

## Technical Background

Jannsen is a Splunk administrator and observability engineer with more than
eight years of enterprise technology experience, including approximately seven
years focused on Splunk administration and development. His work has covered
Splunk platform operations, data onboarding, SPL, dashboards, reports, alerts,
forwarders, application support, upgrades, cloud-migration support, incident and
change management, and technical documentation.

He has worked across enterprise environments where operational correctness,
clear handoffs, controlled changes, and supportable documentation matter. He
also has experience leading a small team, mentoring engineers, and teaching
Splunk practices internally.

## Learning Philosophy

Learning should produce a working mental model, not just a successful command.
Jannsen values explanations that connect an action to the system behavior behind
it. A failure is useful when its conditions, cause, correction, and validation
are recorded honestly.

Prefer a small manual implementation that exposes the mechanism before adding
automation. Preserve useful troubleshooting context, but promote only durable
knowledge into canonical documentation.

## Engineering Style

Jannsen works evidence first. He prefers to inspect the current state, define a
narrow change, make the smallest useful modification, and validate the result
before expanding scope. He values clear role boundaries, conservative security
decisions, reproducibility, maintainability, and honest status labels.

He would rather publish a narrow capability that is proven than a broad claim
that cannot be reviewed. Simplicity is preferred when it makes the system easier
to understand and operate; complexity must earn its place.

## Preferred Workflow

Begin with the repository, not memory or prior chat. Read the active planning
records, confirm the authorized task, inspect the affected system, and state the
validation approach. Work in one logical change at a time. Capture evidence and
update the correct canonical owner. Review the diff and validation results with
Jannsen before committing.

Jannsen retains authority over scope, architecture, priority, acceptance,
commits, and publication. AI should surface options and trade-offs without
quietly making those decisions for him.

## Preferred Documentation Style

Write in direct, conversational engineering language. Start with the problem or
result, explain why it matters, and show the evidence. Use short paragraphs,
meaningful headings, active voice, and explicit limitations. Avoid promotional
copy, inflated adjectives, and narrative drama.

Where a canonical record already owns a fact, link to it instead of restating a
second version. Follow [Writing Conventions](WRITING_CONVENTIONS.md) and the
repository [Documentation Rules](../docs/documentation/DOCUMENTATION_RULES.md).

## Long-Term Vision for Atlas

Atlas is intended to become a durable engineering publication and a practical
systems lab. It should let another engineer follow the evolution of a real
Splunk environment from architecture and configuration through runtime evidence,
decisions, failures, validation, and lessons.

The planned direction includes a read-only Atlas MCP Platform and Configuration
Intelligence, but planning is not completion. The [Roadmap](../ROADMAP.md)
describes direction, [Milestones](../docs/milestones.md) own validation state,
and the [Active Batch](../docs/planning/ACTIVE_BATCH.md) alone authorizes current
execution. Future AI integrations must strengthen evidence and human judgment,
not replace them.
